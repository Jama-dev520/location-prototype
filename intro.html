01 · The problem

Four jobs. Everything in the screen is a consequence of one of them.

1Know what exists

Providers ship us a directory every night. Nobody typed it, nobody can fix it by hand at our end.

2Decide what customers see

Not every location should go live. A go-live is a decision with a name and a time attached to it.

3Keep it true

The provider changes a street or an opening time. The customer must not be left with yesterday's answer.

4Prove who decided

When someone asks "why is this store live with a wrong postcode", the answer has to be a record, not a memory.

The console is small because most of the work is not ours to do: the data belongs to the provider, the decision belongs to us, and the audit belongs to both.

Land this hard: we are not building a data editor, we are building a decision surface for data we do not own.

03 · One value, three sources

Source and type are the same fact — so we print them once.

What you see in the list

PUDOpoint (PUDO)  packagehub (LMS)  M&MP

The type is the value. The parenthesis is who owns it. A self-operated store prints once, because both halves are the same word.

Why not two columns

A source can only produce its own type: LMS ships package hubs, PUDO Inc ships points, M&MP is our own shop. Two columns said the same thing twice, plus a badge for when they disagreed.

04 · The two verbs

Operators can do exactly two things.

Enable a store, or flag a problem. There is no third verb, and there is no edit box.

Every field on this screen is read-only. If a street is wrong, typing over it would make our copy disagree with the provider's — and the next nightly sync would either overwrite our correction or quietly fork the truth. So the console asks the owner to fix it, at the source, and keeps the record of having asked.

enable

Publishes the store to customers. Requires a sign-off: the values are read back, four checks must pass, and the operator ticks a box that says "I looked at this".

flag

Records what is wrong — field, sub-field, type, severity, reason — on the vendor record. Nothing changes value. The provider is now on the hook, and the flag closes when the fix arrives.

Up is a check

Blocking issues keep the button disabled — not a warning you can click through. A store cannot go live on a guess.

Down is one click

Taking a store offline never needs a reason box. The asymmetry is deliberate: a wrong removal costs a customer one search, a wrong publication costs trust.

The baseline is the reference point for everything that comes later. Without it, "the provider changed something" is a rumour; with it, it is a diff.

If someone asks why blocking exists at all: because the failure is asymmetric. Hiding a good store is recoverable, publishing a store with no postcode is not.
06 · After go-live

The provider's new value always wins. Drift is information, not an approval.

The rule

A sync writes what the provider sent — straight through to the downstream. There is no accept / reject, no re-sign. The console is not a gate on data quality; it is a record of it.

The consequence

So "drift" cannot mean "pending approval". It means: this store is live, and three fields have moved since we signed off. The customer already has the new values — approving them would be approving the past.

What we do with it

Look. If the new value is wrong, flag it and the loop starts. If it is fine, nothing to do — and the baseline can be refreshed by the next sign-off.

This is the least intuitive part of the design, and the one that removes the most work: nobody approves a value that has already shipped.

Location Management Console
Common objection: "so wrong data can go live?" Answer: yes — and it would go live anyway the moment the provider published it. What we control is the flag and the chase, not the feed.

What closes itself

Missing, malformed, self-contradictory, out of range. The fix is mechanical, so the console can verify it.

What comes back to a human

Incorrect, stale, duplicate. Those are judgements — a machine can confirm a value arrived, not that it is the right shop.

08 · The ledger

One list of everything wrong — no review queues.

Split by nature

Missing critical fields — nothing arrived where a customer-facing field must exist. Blocking by default.

Inaccurate data — a value is there and it is wrong. Needs a judgement.

The two ask for different people and close by different rules; mixing them lets people assume everything is "waiting for the provider".

Four checks that must not slide

Incomplete address2
No postcode2
No opening hours3
Day under 3 hours3

The last one needs no flag: it is arithmetic on the ranges. A staffed counter open two hours is a truncated feed, not a rota.

Each check is derived from the field, so a new flag lands in the right bucket without anyone choosing a category — the count and the list can never disagree.

11 · Roles

Two roles, and the line is drawn where a decision starts.

Viewer

  • Read every list and detail page
  • Export CSV
  • Cannot enable, flag, decide a duplicate, or pull the feed

Location Manager

  • Everything the Viewer can do
  • Enable and disable, single or batch
  • Flag issues, decide duplicates, pull the provider feed

A pull is a Manager action, not a scheduled one, because it writes provider values — even though the nightly job does exactly the same thing with nobody watching. The rule is about who can change data, not about who can look.
